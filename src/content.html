Featured case · DMAIC

Lab consumables, fixed after years of false starts

Earlier attempts started inside one lab and stopped at the next function's door. I set this one up across functions from day one. Details are generalized: no proprietary, client or personnel information.

D

Define. Scientists and technicians spent over 80 hours a week finding, ordering and tracking supplies, across five labs with no system. Chartered with an owner in each function and sponsorship above them.

M

Measure. Walked five current-state maps with the people doing the work. Baseline: 110 hrs/week, 600+ SKUs with no signal, 12+ storage locations.

A

Analyze. One supplier base, one dock, one budget, and no common method. The inventory was never the problem. The missing system was.

I

Improve. Two kanban loops sized by demand: re-stock cards for fast movers, re-order cards for slow ones. 625 cards generated by code from the item master.

C

Control. Live stock and expiry in Power BI, min/max re-sizing flags, standard work, and a kamishibai audit. It now runs without me.

Data infrastructure · Power BI2026 · in lab review, going live next

Lab sample turnaround: from a dozen Excel trackers to one live view

Analysts kept 10 to 12 Excel trackers up to date by hand, copying status out of the lab notebook (ELN) and LIMS. Nobody could say how long a sample took from receipt to QA review. I mapped the sample journey step by step, found the timestamp each system already records, and designed a pipeline so the numbers come from the systems themselves.

ELN + LIMSsource systems
Daily extractread-only, one row per event
Datalakejoined on sample, ELN, batch
Power BIone semantic model
Live pageslabs, QA, leadership

Ten pages, one question each

  1. Which samples are open, and which are oldest?
  2. Where is a given sample right now?
  3. Is turnaround improving, and which stage holds the time?
  4. Which tests are slowest: waiting or testing?
  5. What test work is open?
  6. What is in cold storage, and what can be disposed?
  7. What stability pulls are coming?
  8. Which batches are waiting to release?
  9. How much testing comes due each week?
  10. How many instrument runs do we need?

Problem solved along the way

LIMS doesn't record instrument runs. So I inferred them: tests of the same method and project peer-reviewed on the same day count as one run, and run size is the 12-month median. Equipment maximums override history when they are smaller. That turns a sample backlog into the number of runs a lab actually has to schedule.

How turnaround is measured

Median and 80th-percentile days from receipt to QA review, % within a 30-day target, and a stage waterfall that shows where the days go.
